# How to Personalise Relay Solutions Outreach Emails

A step-by-step guide for personalising the trades outreach template — written so it can be followed exactly, with no guesswork, by a future AI or a human doing this manually.

---

## 1. The Golden Rule

**Only two things change per email: the greeting name, and the first sentence's specific detail. Nothing else in the body copy changes.**

This is the single most important rule and the one most likely to get broken. The template has been tested and approved. Every other line — the "genie" line, the free audit pitch, the closing — stays word-for-word identical every time. Do not:

- Rewrite sentences "to flow better"
- Swap words for synonyms
- Add or remove paragraphs
- Change the structure or order
- "Improve" the tone

If you think a sentence could be better, that's not your call to make here. The person approved this copy. Your only job is the personalisation slot.

Everything between "But I bet you hate all the admin" and the sign-off is fixed. Copy it exactly.

## 3. Where the Personalisation Comes From

The personalised detail must be **specific and true** — something that could only apply to this business, not a generic trades compliment. Think: "a reclaimed two-storey extension with salvaged brickwork," not "amazing building work."

### Research workflow (do this for every prospect, in order):

1. **Search the business name + website domain** (e.g. `"JBT Renovations" jbtrenovations.co.uk`). This surfaces the business's own site content, directory listings, and review platforms in one pass.
2. **Prioritise these page types when they show up in results:**
   - "Projects," "Portfolio," "Gallery," "Our Work" — best source, shows actual completed jobs
   - "About Us" — often has founding story, years of experience, specialism
   - "Services" — tells you what they actually do (roofing vs. loft conversions vs. dry stone walling), useful if projects aren't available
   - Google/Facebook reviews — sometimes name specific jobs or a named team member
3. **Look for something concrete and describable in one phrase:**
   - A specific completed project ("a reclaimed two-storey extension with salvaged brickwork")
   - A clear specialism ("dry stone walling," "sandstone restoration," "loft conversions")
   - A notable fact (years trading, founding story, a specific technique)
4. **If nothing specific and verifiable turns up after 1–2 searches, STOP searching that prospect and move on.** Don't burn excessive time hunting for a detail that isn't there — some businesses (especially ones with generic names like "Cornerstone" or minimal web presence) simply won't have findable info.

### What counts as "specific enough" vs. not:

| Good (use it) | Bad (don't use it) |
|---|---|
| "flat roofs, sandstone restoration, the lot across Falkirk" | "amazing roofing services" |
| "a reclaimed two-storey extension with salvaged brickwork" | "great extension work" |
| "dry stone walling across Northumberland" | "quality building work" |
| "extensions, loft conversions, even heritage and listed building projects" | "years of experience" |

The test: **could this sentence apply to literally any builder in the UK?** If yes, it's too generic — either dig one search deeper or treat it as "no info found."

## 4. What To Do When You Can't Find Anything

This will happen regularly — many small trades businesses have thin or no web presence beyond a Google Business listing and a Facebook page with no real content.

**Do not fabricate a detail.** Never invent a project, a technique, or a fact that isn't backed by something you actually found. This is the single worst mistake possible — a fabricated detail that turns out to be wrong (or that the recipient can tell is generic/fake) destroys credibility instantly and makes the whole outreach look like spam.

When no real detail is found, you have two options:
1. **Flag it and skip that prospect**, moving to the next one, and tell the person clearly you skipped it and why.
2. **If instructed to send regardless**, fall back to the truly generic version of the line: *"the great work you put out there"* (no specific detail inserted) — this is the safe default the template already supports, since it doesn't claim anything false.

Never present option 2 as if it were option 1. Always be upfront about which one you're using.

## 5. Full List of Things Not To Do

These are mistakes that happened or nearly happened while building this process — treat this as a checklist to actively avoid:

1. **Don't rewrite the approved copy.** Even small wording "improvements" are not requested and not wanted. If the person wants a copy change, they'll ask for it explicitly.
2. **Don't invent, guess, or assume details about a business's work.** Only use what you actually found in a search result.
3. **Don't use vague filler as if it were personalisation.** "Great work," "clearly skilled," "impressive business" are not personalisation — they're what the template already says by default. The personalised detail has to be a specific noun phrase describing real work.
4. **Don't over-research.** One or two well-chosen searches per prospect is enough. If it's not turning up on the first pass, it's usually not there — move on rather than exhaustively digging.
5. **Don't confuse similarly-named businesses.** Generic names ("Cornerstone," "Prime," "Cheshire's Friendly Builders") often collide with unrelated companies, churches, charities, or restaurants in search results. If you can't confirm you've found the *right* business's actual site, treat it as "no info found" rather than guessing.
6. **Don't change the subject line format.** Keep it short and specific: `[What you did], [First Name]` (e.g. "Loved your roofing work, Anthony"). Don't make it clickbaity, don't add emojis to the subject line, don't make it vague ("Quick question" is the kind of subject line to avoid — it's overused and looks like spam).
7. **Don't mix up contact details between prospects.** Always double check the email address, name, and business name are from the *same row* in the source data before sending. A mismatched name/email pairing is an easy, embarrassing error.
8. **Don't process more prospects than requested in one go**, and don't skip ahead without being asked — go one at a time if that's what's been asked for, so each one can be checked.
9. **Don't add unsolicited extra content** (P.S. lines, extra offers, additional CTAs) — stick to exactly what the template has.
10. **Don't use information that's sensitive, embarrassing, or could read as creepy/stalker-ish** — e.g. don't reference personal social media, family details, or anything beyond publicly published business/trade work. Stick strictly to their professional output.

## 6. Standard Operating Procedure (Step by Step)

For each new prospect, follow this exact sequence:

1. Pull the row from the CSV: **First name, business name, email, website.**
2. Run one search: `"[Business Name]" [website domain]`
3. Scan results for a specific, real, describable piece of work or clear specialism (see Section 3's good/bad table).
4. If found: write a short phrase (5–12 words) describing it in plain language.
5. If not found after 1–2 searches: flag as "no specific info found" and either skip or use the generic fallback line — tell the person which.
6. Drop the first name into the greeting and the phrase into the personalisation slot. **Do not touch any other line.**
7. Write a short, specific subject line: `[What you did], [First Name]`.
8. Present as: **email address, subject line, then the full email copy** — this exact order, every time, so it's easy to copy-paste into an email client.
9. Move to the next prospect only when asked, unless told to batch-process.

---

## 7. Quality Checklist (Before Sending Any Email)

- [ ] Is the greeting name correct and matched to the right email address?
- [ ] Is the personalised detail specific, true, and traceable to something actually found online (not invented)?
- [ ] Does the personalised detail avoid generic phrases like "great work" or "impressive service"?
- [ ] Is every other line identical, word-for-word, to the locked template?
- [ ] Is the subject line short, specific, and not clickbaity?
- [ ] Are the email address, subject, and body all for the *same* business/person?
